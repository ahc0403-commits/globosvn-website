Rebuild the Globos Holdings website around one clear position: Vietnam market entry that continues into local operation, store control, distribution, ecommerce, and measurable expansion.

## Benchmark Matrix

| Group | Reference Pattern | Useful for Globos | What to Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market-entry advisory | Clear service routes for incorporation, licensing, tax, HR, compliance, and post-entry support | Use concrete entry problems such as company setup, licensing, registration, customs, and partner search | Generic consulting language without operating evidence |
| Vietnam specialists | Local risk framing around restrictions, entity type, capital, permits, and administrative timing | Show that Vietnam entry is not one document but a sequence of local decisions | Legal-heavy copy that makes Globos look like a law firm |
| Restaurant POS platforms | Dashboard-first visuals, live numbers, store status, order flow, inventory, labor, settlement | Make Globos feel like an operating control system, not a brochure | Fake stock photos or unrelated business imagery |
| Delivery integrations | POS, delivery apps, menu, order, and settlement connection | Explain why POS, ERP, delivery, ecommerce, and reporting should be designed together | Treating delivery as a marketing add-on |
| Ecommerce expansion | Localized market, channel, currency, logistics, and checkout/control from one backend | Use Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop, seller operation, content, CS, and settlement as search-intent routes | Broad "online marketing" wording with no channel specificity |
| Franchise directories | Fast route matching between brand type, market need, and next action | Let visitors identify their own situation before reading service details | Too many internal service names before the visitor understands fit |

## Home Structure Options

### Option A: Advisory-Led

1. Hero: Vietnam entry problem.
2. Why it is hard: legal, licensing, distribution, store operation.
3. Service routes: franchise, beauty, F&B, ecommerce, sourcing.
4. Proof: stores, partners, systems, 2030 vision.
5. Organization: founders and operating teams.
6. SEO landing links.

Strength: simple and consulting-friendly.

Weakness: Globos may look like another advisory firm.

### Option B: Operating Control Tower

1. Hero: local operating team for brands entering Vietnam.
2. Control tower: setup, operate, scale in one route.
3. Bottlenecks: legal, store, distribution, systems.
4. Visitor routes: franchise HQ, cosmetics, F&B, ecommerce, operations, sourcing.
5. Operating system: ERP, SaaS POS, delivery, dashboards.
6. Execution flows: franchise, cosmetics, store operation, marketing/ecommerce.
7. Proof and vision: 2024 start, 8 stores, 20+ partner firms, 2030 target of 300 stores and 50 partners.
8. Mission, vision, leadership, organization.
9. Service map and SEO landing routes.

Strength: combines the credibility of market-entry firms with the confidence of operating platforms.

Weakness: requires strong visual discipline and concise writing so the story does not feel crowded.

## Selected Direction

Option B is selected.

Globos should not lead as a consulting menu. It should lead as a Vietnam operating control tower: define the entry route, execute locally, measure through systems, and expand with the 2030 platform vision.

## Copy Direction

Korean copy should sound like a founder explaining the business clearly, not like translated SaaS copy. The homepage can use sentence endings, but the visible rhythm should stay compact: problem, evidence, operating route, next action.

## Visual Direction

Use no people photos. Use:

- Operating dashboards with revenue, profit, store status, orders, inventory, and settlement.
- Legal and licensing imagery only for legal sections.
- Route maps, flow charts, and matrices for Korea to Vietnam entry.
- Blurred numbers, charts, tables, and document fragments that look like real business material.
- Subtle motion for dashboard changes and process flow.
