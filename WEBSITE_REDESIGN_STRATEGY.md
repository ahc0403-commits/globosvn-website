# Globos Holdings Website Redesign Strategy

## Objective

Reposition the Globos Holdings website from a general corporate profile into a search-driven, sales-oriented website for Vietnam market entry, franchise expansion, store operations, distribution, sourcing, legal setup, marketing localization, and operating infrastructure.

The site should communicate one core idea:

> Globos Holdings is not a presentation-only consulting firm. It is a Vietnam execution partner already operating stores, systems, and local infrastructure.

## Strategic Positioning

Globos Holdings should be framed as a Vietnam-based execution platform for Korean and international brands that need more than advice.

The public message should be:

- We began operating in Vietnam in 2024.
- We currently operate 8 stores in Vietnam.
- We plan to expand to 20 stores within the year.
- We have built our own operating infrastructure, including ERP, SaaS POS, store operation workflows, and delivery app infrastructure.
- We support market entry, franchise expansion, F&B entry, retail operations, local licensing, sourcing, distribution, marketing, and technology infrastructure as one connected execution system.

This proof-led positioning is the main difference from ordinary market-entry consultants.

## Visual Design Direction

Use the redesign to feel like a premium execution platform, not a generic consulting site.

The website should rely on strong visual proof: business-relevant images, animated diagrams, moving data graphics, operating maps, and infrastructure charts. The goal is to make the company feel active, operational, and already in motion.

### Typography

- Display: `Clash Display` for high-impact headlines.
- Body/UI: `Plus Jakarta Sans` for clear business reading.
- Editorial accent: `PP Editorial` or another distinctive serif for selective quotes and leadership statements.

### Layout Rhythm

Do not repeat the same centered section pattern throughout the page. Rotate:

- Split-screen hero.
- Modular bento proof section.
- Layered depth stage for operating infrastructure.
- Editorial asymmetric customer entry section.
- Cinematic leadership sequence.
- Brutalist-refined SEO gateway.

### Material System

- Core ink: deep blue-black rather than flat black.
- Background: white, mist gray, and cool industrial surfaces.
- Accent: restrained brass or signal blue used sparingly.
- Texture: subtle grain at low opacity.
- Depth: overlapping planes and directional lighting, not decorative shadows alone.

### Image and Visualization System

Use visuals that explain the business, not generic decoration.

Hard image rules:

- No people in any website image unless the user later provides explicit approved real photography.
- Do not use AI-looking imagery. Avoid synthetic faces, distorted hands, fake office scenes, overly glossy 3D renders, surreal lighting, impossible architecture, unreadable fake text, and stock-photo smiles.
- Prefer object-led, environment-led, and data-led visuals: documents, contracts, maps, counters, POS screens, delivery route diagrams, dashboards, inventory flows, operating charts, shelves, storefront details, packaging, logistics surfaces, legal files, and abstracted system interfaces.
- If generated images are used, they should look like premium editorial photography or real business documentation photographed with controlled lighting, shallow depth of field, natural imperfections, and believable materials.
- Text inside generated images should be avoided unless it is intentionally blurred or abstracted; real site text should be HTML, SVG, or canvas.

Recommended assets:

- Store operations photography or generated imagery showing counters, menu boards without readable fake text, POS terminals, packaging, shelves, receipts, kitchen/service surfaces, and retail execution details without people.
- Dashboard-style images with out-of-focus numbers, sales charts, inventory flows, and operating metrics.
- Vietnam market maps showing store footprint and future expansion paths.
- Infrastructure diagrams showing ERP, POS, delivery app, store reporting, and data feedback loops.
- Service ecosystem diagrams showing how legal, sourcing, franchise, marketing, IT, and operations connect.
- Organization chart graphics for leadership and team structure.

Section-specific visual concepts:

- Legal / licensing difficulty: close-up contract pages, stamped documents, law-book textures, clause highlights, and layered compliance checklists. No lawyers, no courtroom people.
- Korea-to-Vietnam brand movement: map from Korea to Vietnam with animated routes, arrow trails, store nodes, and service layers appearing along the route.
- Franchise expansion: brand system node splitting into Store 01, Store 02, Store 08, and Target 20 nodes.
- Distribution / logistics: Vietnam map, supplier nodes, warehouse blocks, route lines, shipping labels, inventory surfaces, and delivery path animation.
- Store operations: POS terminal, order tickets, shelf layouts, packaging, counter detail, dashboard overlay, and operational scorecards.
- ERP / SaaS POS / delivery app: UI-inspired dashboard panels, data streams, order flow graph, multi-store reporting grid, and system integration diagram.
- Leadership / organization: no portraits. Use initials, role cards, operating credentials, and an org chart that connects co-founders to operating teams.

Dynamic visualization ideas:

- Count-up proof cards for `8 operating stores` and `20 target stores`.
- animated line chart for store expansion from 2024 to the current year and next target.
- Animated stack diagram for `ERP -> POS -> Store Operations -> Delivery App -> Reporting`.
- Motion map with store nodes pulsing across Vietnam.
- Scroll-triggered service network diagram where each business unit activates in sequence.

Avoid:

- Executive headshots or any person-centered image unless real approved photography is provided later.
- Any image where people, faces, hands, or body parts are visible.
- AI-generated imagery that looks too perfect, plastic, surreal, or obviously synthetic.
- Unrelated skyscraper, handshake, or generic consulting imagery.
- Static blocks of text where a graph, map, or system diagram can explain the point faster.

### Motion

- Scroll reveals should be restrained and staggered.
- Hover states should change position and surface treatment together.
- Data/proof cards should feel alive through subtle number emphasis and micro-motion.
- Charts and maps should move with purpose: draw lines, count numbers, reveal systems, or show expansion. Avoid decorative motion that does not explain the operating story.
